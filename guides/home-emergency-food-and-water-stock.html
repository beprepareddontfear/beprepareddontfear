How do I build a home emergency food and water stock?

Store about 1 gallon (3.8 litres) of water per person per day for several days, plus a few days of food your household already eats that needs no fridge. Rotate it, keep it cool and dry, and review it once a year.

How much food and water do I need for 3 days?

Ready.gov, the US government's preparedness site, advises about one gallon of water per person per day for several days, and at least a several-day supply of non-perishable food.

The cheapest way to build the stock is to buy a little extra of what you already eat:

  • Rice, pasta, dried beans or lentils
  • Canned fish, meat, vegetables and fruit
  • Sugar, honey, salt, cooking oil
  • Tea, coffee, powdered or long-life milk
  • Anything your family already eats that needs no fridge

For a family of four, 3 days of water is about 12 gallons (45 litres). Fill clean containers before you need them and store them cool and dark.

How long do pantry foods last?

Most dry staples keep 1 to 2 years if sealed and kept cool and dry. Two university guides give different figures, so use the shorter one when in doubt.

Both guides call these guidelines, not hard rules. Heat and humidity shorten them. Past the date, food is often still safe but loses flavour, texture and nutrients. Wholemeal flour, brown rice and oils go off sooner because of their natural oils.

How do I store emergency food so it lasts?

  • Where: a cool, dry cupboard away from the oven, stove, fridge back and hot pipes. Heat turns oils rancid. Keep items off the floor.
  • Temperature: New Mexico State University says about 50°F (10°C) is ideal and 60-70°F (15-21°C) is acceptable. Clemson says 65-70°F (18-21°C).
  • Containers: tightly closed glass, metal or rigid plastic, or zip bags once packs are opened.
  • Rotation: write the purchase date on each item and eat the oldest first, then replace it.
  • Throw away cans or packs that leak, bulge, are badly dented, rusted or cracked, and any food with a bad smell or colour change. The rule is: when in doubt, throw it out.
  • Check once a year: Ready.gov advises reviewing the kit yearly and replacing expired items.

What goes in a homemade emergency kit?

  • Water and a few days of non-perishable food
  • Torch and spare batteries
  • Battery or hand-crank radio
  • Phone charger and a power bank
  • First-aid kit and any regular medicines
  • Whistle to signal for help
  • Manual can opener
  • Wipes, rubbish bags and ties for sanitation
  • Dust masks, scissors and duct tape
  • Local paper map and a written list of key phone numbers
  • Copies of ID and important papers in a waterproof bag, plus some cash
What not to improvise. Home canning of low-acid foods such as vegetables and meat can cause botulism, even when the food looks and smells fine. Learn canning only from tested procedures such as those published by the USDA. For water treatment and medical care, follow your local health authority.
