How do I store drinking water for an emergency?

Store at least 1 gallon (3.8 litres) of water per person per day for 3 days, ideally 2 weeks, in clean, food-grade containers kept cool and dark, and replace home-stored water every 6 months.

How much water should I store?

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) says to store at least 1 gallon per person per day, for 3 days. A 2-week supply is recommended if you can manage it. Store more for pregnant women, people who are sick, pets, or if you live in a hot climate.

What is the safest water to store?

Unopened, commercially bottled water is the safest and most reliable option. Follow the expiry date on the bottle. For water you store yourself, the CDC says to replace it every 6 months.

Which containers can I use?

  • Best: food-grade water storage containers.
  • If you cannot get those: choose a container with a tight-closing lid that is durable and unbreakable, ideally with a narrow opening. Do not use glass.
  • Never use containers that held toxic chemicals such as bleach or pesticides.

How do I clean a container before filling it?

  1. Wash it with soap and rinse well.
  2. Mix 1 teaspoon of unscented liquid household chlorine bleach (5-9% sodium hypochlorite) into 1 quart (4 cups) of water.
  3. Cover the container tightly and shake so the solution reaches every inside surface.
  4. Wait at least 30 seconds, then pour the solution out.
  5. Let it air-dry, fill it with clean water and seal it.

Where should I keep stored water?

  • Cool, ideally 50-70°F (10-21°C), and out of direct sunlight.
  • Away from gasoline, pesticides and other toxic substances.
  • Label each container "drinking water" with the date you filled it.
  • Take water out with a clean scoop or tap. Never dip your hands in.
Making water safe. This guide covers storage only. For how to disinfect water in an emergency, follow the CDC page "Making Water Safe in an Emergency" or your local health authority, and keep unscented household bleach on hand for it.
