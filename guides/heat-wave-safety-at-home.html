How do I stay safe at home during a heat wave?

The CDC says to drink more water than usual without waiting to feel thirsty, wear light loose clothing, limit midday activity and stove use, and use air-conditioned places if your home has no cooling. Never leave children or pets in a car, and check on people at higher risk.

Is a fan enough?

The CDC says not to rely on a fan as your main cooling device during an extreme heat event. If you have no air conditioning, use an air-conditioned public place or ask your local health department about cooling shelters.

Who is most at risk?

  • People aged 65 and older
  • Children under two
  • People with chronic diseases or mental illness

Other factors include dehydration, fever, heart disease, sunburn, and alcohol or some prescription drugs. Check on people who depend on you: are they drinking enough, and can they keep cool?

What about children and pets?

Never leave children or pets in a parked car.

When should I get medical help?

If you or someone else shows signs of heat-related illness, seek medical care right away. The CDC publishes a separate page on the warning signs.

What can I prepare before a heat wave?

  • Store extra drinking water; see our water storage guide.
  • Find your nearest cooled public place.
  • Plan for power cuts that stop fans and cooling; see our power cut plan.
Note. General information only, not medical advice. Heat can be dangerous. Follow local health authority warnings.
