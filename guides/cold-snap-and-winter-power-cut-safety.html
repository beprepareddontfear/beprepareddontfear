How do I stay warm and safe in a cold snap or winter power cut?

Keep heat in by closing off rooms and covering windows, dress in layers, and never run generators, grills or camp stoves indoors because the fumes can kill. Keep space heaters 3 feet from anything flammable and install a battery-operated carbon monoxide alarm.

How do I keep a home warm without much heat?

  • Close off rooms you do not need.
  • Stuff towels under doors.
  • Close curtains or cover windows at night.
  • Dress in layers: a wicking inner layer, an insulating middle layer and a wind- and water-resistant outer layer.
  • Eat well and drink warm, sweet drinks or broth. Avoid alcohol and caffeine, which make you lose heat faster.

How do I avoid carbon monoxide?

RuleFrom the CDC
GeneratorsAt least 20 feet from any window, door or vent, and sheltered from rain and snow
Grills and camp stovesNever use gas or charcoal grills or camp stoves indoors, in basements, garages or near windows
CookerDo not use your stove for heat
AlarmInstall a battery-operated CO detector
Fuel heatersUse fireplaces, wood stoves and combustion heaters only if vented outside

How do I use space heaters safely?

  • Keep them at least 3 feet from drapes, furniture and bedding.
  • Choose models with automatic shut-off.
  • Do not use extension cords, and stop using a heater with a damaged cord or sparks.
  • Have your heating system serviced by a qualified technician every year.

What are the warning signs of hypothermia and frostbite?

  • Hypothermia in adults: shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, memory loss, slurred speech, drowsiness.
  • In infants: bright red, cold skin and very low energy.
  • Frostbite: white or grey-yellow skin, unusually firm or waxy skin, numbness, usually on nose, ears, cheeks, chin, fingers and toes.

A body temperature below 95°F (35°C) is an emergency needing immediate medical attention. Seek medical care for frostbite.

Who needs extra care?

Infants under one year should never sleep in a cold room. Remove pillows and soft bedding and dress them in warm sleepwear. Check on older neighbours often.
