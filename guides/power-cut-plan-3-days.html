What should I do during a power cut? A 3-day plan

Keep the fridge and freezer closed, have light, a radio and a charged phone ready, never use a gas stove or oven for heat, and throw away food that sat above 40°F (4°C) for two hours or more.

What should I prepare before a power cut?

  • Water and food that needs no fridge or cooking (outages can also make local water unsafe to drink)
  • Torches and spare batteries for everyone, or rechargeable lanterns and headlamps
  • A battery-powered or hand-crank radio
  • Offline activities that need no power: puzzles, games, music on a battery radio
  • A plan to keep medicine cold if someone needs refrigerated medicine
  • Backup batteries for any powered medical machine or wheelchair, and a neighbour, friend or relative you can stay with

What should I do when the power goes out?

  • Keep the fridge and freezer closed. A fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours. A full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours.
  • Use a generator only outdoors, away from windows.
  • Never heat your home with a gas stove or oven. It can cause carbon monoxide poisoning.
  • Unplug the fridge and electronics, so a surge does not damage them when power returns (an adult should do this).
  • Too hot or too cold? Check local news for open heating or cooling locations.
  • Need power for medical equipment? Go somewhere safer, such as a neighbour's, friend's or relative's home.

What should I do when the power comes back?

  • Check that everything you unplugged works.
  • Throw away food that was at 40°F (4°C) or warmer for two hours or more, or that smells or looks different. When in doubt, throw it out.
  • If the outage lasted more than a day, throw away refrigerated medicine unless its label says it is still safe. Contact a doctor or pharmacist for a replacement.

A simple 3-day plan

WhenWhat to do
Hour 0-4Keep the fridge shut, find your torch and radio, charge phones while you can, check on neighbours.
Hour 4-48Eat fridge food first, then freezer food. Use water from your stored supply.
Day 2-3Move to non-perishable food, ration phone use, and decide whether to stay with a friend or relative if it is too hot or cold.
Note. The plan above applies the times Ready.gov gives for fridges and freezers. Real times vary with how full they are and how warm the room is.
