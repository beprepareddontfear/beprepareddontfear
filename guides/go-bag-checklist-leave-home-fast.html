What should I pack in a go bag to leave home fast?

A go bag holds what you need for up to three days away: clothes, water and food, copies of key documents, prescription medicines, some cash and chargers. Keep it where you can grab it on the way out, and check it twice a year.

What goes in a go bag?

This list follows University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension guidance for a go bag kept at home. Adjust it to your family, your climate and your pets.

GroupWhat to pack
ClothingA change of clothes for each person, rain gear, blankets or sleeping bags
Food and waterWater and food for people and pets for up to three days. Choose non-perishables such as energy bars, dried fruit, nuts, crackers, jerky and peanut butter. Add cups, plates, utensils and a can opener
DocumentsPassports, birth certificates, children's immunization records, copies of prescriptions, insurance policy numbers, utility and bank contacts, pet vaccination records
MedicinesPrescription medicines, kept where you can grab them on the way out
CashCash for when cards or machines do not work
Children and petsBooks or games for children, supplies and medicines for pets
PhoneCharger and power bank (the same source lists these for a car kit; if you have no car, keep them in the bag)

How much cash should I keep?

The extension rule of thumb is about 50 to 100 US dollars a day for food, fuel and possibly lodging. Scale it to your family size and your local currency, and keep it somewhere hard for thieves to find.

How do I keep documents safe?

  • Use a fireproof or waterproof box, or keep copies electronically on your phone.
  • Save doctor and pharmacy contacts in your phone.

Is a go bag different from a stay-at-home kit?

Yes. A go bag is light enough to carry and meant for leaving. A stay bin is a larger store for sheltering at home, where the same source suggests at least 1 gallon (about 3.8 litres) of water per person per day. See our home food and water guide.

Note. This guide gives no medical advice. Ask your doctor or pharmacist how to store and carry your own medicines. In an emergency, follow instructions from local authorities.
